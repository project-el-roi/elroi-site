Why now

The dry season is arriving early and El Niño is arriving behind it.

Karamoja, in Uganda's far northeast, is semi-arid and agro-pastoralist: families live off what they plant at the rains and what their herds survive. When the rains fail, both fail together. Hunger in the region has repeatedly peaked during drought, and an El Niño cycle on top of a poor harvest compounds it — the World Food Programme has reported on this pattern in Karamoja directly.

What that means in Logoole is narrower and more immediate: households run out of food before the next planting, eat their seed stock to survive, and then have nothing to sow when the rain does come. That is the loop this project is trying to interrupt.

Giving

We are not taking card payments yet, and here is why.

Right now, support reaches Logoole as mobile money sent to the pastor, who buys posho and beans at the local market, cooks, serves, and writes up a feeding report. It works, it is fast, and it is how 228 people ate on the last recorded day.

What it is not, yet, is a system a stranger should wire money into. There is no registered account, no independent bookkeeping, and no audited trail of daily giving. Putting a donate button on this page before that exists would be asking for trust we have not built. So the button is not here.
