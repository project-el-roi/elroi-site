About

One problem rarely stays one problem.

El Roi is the name Hagar gave God in the desert: the God who sees me. Karamoja is a place the rest of the country forgets between droughts. The point of this project is to be a record that somebody is watching, and then does something about it.

  1. Drought and food insecurity
  2. Hunger
  3. Health, education and household pressures
  4. Reduced stability
  5. Need for longer-term resilience

These are connected needs, not a chain in which one step guarantees the next. The response is organised in three parts.

Respond

Food, water, baby supplies, clothing and basic support.

Restore

Education, health, hygiene and young mother support.

Build

Skills, seeds, food production and food security.

Why now

The dry season is arriving early and El Niño is arriving behind it.

Karamoja, in Uganda's far northeast, is semi-arid and agro-pastoralist: families live off what they plant at the rains and what their herds survive. When the rains fail, both fail together. Hunger in the region has repeatedly peaked during drought, and an El Niño cycle on top of a poor harvest compounds it — the World Food Programme has reported on this pattern in Karamoja directly.

What that means in Logoole is narrower and more immediate: households run out of food before the next planting, eat their seed stock to survive, and then have nothing to sow when the rain does come. That is the loop this project is trying to interrupt.

Get involved

We are not taking card payments yet, and here is why.

Right now, support reaches Logoole as mobile money sent to the pastor, who buys posho and beans at the local market, cooks, serves, and writes up a feeding report. It works, it is fast, and it is how 228 people ate on the last recorded day.

What it is not, yet, is a system a stranger should wire money into. There is no registered account, no independent bookkeeping, and no audited trail of daily giving. Putting a payment button on this page before that exists would be asking for trust we have not built. So the Donate button does not take money. It opens a message to a person, by email or WhatsApp, and they will tell you how to give.
